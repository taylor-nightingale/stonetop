# Arcana

Magic that has to be worked out before it can be used.

Two quite different things share the word. **Minor arcana** are projects with a thing-you-can-do at
the end of them. **Major arcana** are powerful magic whose strength is unlocked in stages, and which
may prove too much for the person holding it. They are described separately below, because outside
the shared vocabulary they have little in common.

---

## What the word covers

An arcanum is **a discovery with mysteries in it** — an artifact, a place, a scrap of lore, a magical
effect — that provides power only once someone has worked out how to reach it.

There are sixty-four minor and eighteen major. One playbook begins with a major and two minor;
a particular background on another grants one outright. Anyone can acquire them in play, and most are
found as treasure, uncovered in a ruin, or heard in a story.

**The word itself is out-of-fiction.** *Arcanum* is a game term, not something the characters say.
Nobody in Stonetop announces they have found an arcanum; they have found a cracked flute, or a
half-buried bronze plate, or a path through the woods that is not always there.

**Every arcanum is different**, and the rules say so outright. There is no general procedure for using
one, no shared resource, no common cost. Each states its own, and when something is unclear — how it
works, what it can and cannot do — the answer is extrapolated at the table from what has been
established, rather than looked up.

**A character is meant to know what they are getting into.** The GM is told to be generous with
information, because nobody can make a real decision about a season of work or a risk of lasting harm
without it.

**What an arcanum turns out to be is information, not a reward.** It is normally handed over whole
when the arcanum is found — both what it appears to be and what it will do — because a player cannot
judge whether the requirements are worth paying without knowing what they buy. Where it has to be
earned, it is earned by **study**: a good roll gives all of it at once, a mixed one gives the rest
when there has been time to look properly. **What unlocking gates is use.** A GM may choose to delay
a reveal, and a few of the majors in particular lend themselves to it, but concealment is a judgement
call rather than the rule.

## Minor arcana

### A project with a thing-you-can-do at the end

A minor arcanum is described twice over: **what it appears to be**, along with what it will take to
unlock, and **what it turns out to do**. The second description carries **a different name** — *a
cracked flute* is *Dancing Wind Spirit*; *an old scroll case* is *Laoj Davith's Galvanic Infusion* —
so learning what it is includes learning what to call it.

**It has two states and only two.** Locked, it does nothing whatever. Unlocked, it hands over
everything it has at once — a move, sometimes a follower — and asks nothing further. There is no
ongoing cost and no ledger. A locked minor arcanum is a promise; an unlocked one is finished.

### Unlocking is a job of work

The requirements are **a set of boxes to mark**, and marking the last one unlocks the mysteries.
Getting there is not only study. It is as often restoration, construction, acquisition or
negotiation:

- *Carefully clean away the corrosion with proper chemics, without destroying the runes* · *mount the
  spearhead on a shaft of birchwood* · *soak it in your own blood, to claim its power as yours.*
- *Repair the body, taking a full season for an exceptional smith (Value 3)* · *convince your own
  hearth's spirit to inhabit the stove and animate the construct.*
- *A skilled carpenter, good timber (Value 2), a new sail (Value 1), and a season spent on repairs* —
  and **another person** must spend a season learning to sail it.
- *Convince one of the shades to reveal how it was captured.*
- *Translate the letter* · *decipher the Maker-runes* · *it'll take a few weeks of dedicated study.*

So an unlock list can reach into skilled people, purchased goods with stated Values, whole seasons,
another character's time, and conversations with the dead. **One character's arcanum can be the
party's next season of work.**

Two rules ride along with it:

- **The time is not free.** *Don't be surprised if the GM asks you what chores or obligations go unmet
  as you pursue this magical power.*
- **A requirement that says *you risk ___* is a move.** The character Defies Danger unless they can
  find a way to eliminate the risk. *Learning magic is rarely easy or safe.*

And requirements consume: an ingredient spent on an unlock is gone.

### What is true of them

- **They can be taught onward.** A minor arcanum can be shared with another character, or an NPC,
  where it makes sense — and their requirements are not the same ones. Someone shown the ropes by a
  person who already knows it may need days rather than weeks, and may be helped through the risk.
- **Half of them are not objects.** Thirty-one of the sixty-four are **places** — a creepy cave,
  rune-etched pillars, a clear natural pool, a path in the woods — or **stories**: a folktale, a
  prospector's tale. A place has to be returned to in order to be used. A story cannot be lost,
  dropped or stolen.
- **The ones that are objects are ordinary gear in every other respect.** They have weight, they are
  carried or not, and they count against a load like anything else (`outfitting.md`).
- **Several grant a follower** rather than a move, including objects with a will of their own
  (`followers.md`).
- **A character accumulates locked ones they cannot yet act on**, each stalled on a different
  condition — one needs a terrible burn, one needs another character's help while they are busy, one
  needs a lead nobody has. The locked ones are a standing list of named jobs, and **what blocks each
  of them is the interesting part**.

## Major arcana

### Power unlocked in stages, which may be too much for you

A major arcanum is powerful magic — usually an object, sometimes a place, sometimes power infused
into a person — and it is **not a locked box**. **All of it is in play from the first day.** It
already carries a move or two that work, alongside the track that unlocks it, the mysteries that
track leads to, and the ledger of consequences those mysteries will cost.

**The power comes slowly, in stages.** Unlocking usually grants **one** of the arcanum's mysteries
rather than all of them, and at least one arcanum clears its track afterwards so it can be run again
for the next.

**And the unlocking is itself the dangerous part.** One reads *each time you mark 1, ask the GM how
the power goes out of control* — havoc before the power is even yours.

### It is unlocked by use, not by preparation

Where a minor arcanum's list is work done to it, a major arcanum's track is fed by living with it:

*Sheathe the Sword after using it to kill a living, bleeding foe* · *when you are struck by lightning
or an electrical discharge* · *flay the hide from a demon, tan it, and sew it onto the cloak* ·
*reveal a secret to the stones, or they pry one from your mind* · *perform the sacred rites of the
forest witches, alone in the woods under a clear crescent moon.*

### It adds moves, and they are large

Seventeen of the eighteen add at least one move to the character — thirty-nine across them all. They
are not small effects: leave your body and walk the world as a spirit; call up the servants of a god
and treat them as followers; ruin any work of mortal artifice; strike every victim in range at once;
speak a Word that seals a portal.

**The moves are the arcanum.** Once unlocked, a major arcanum is a set of capabilities the character
carries into every scene, not an item consulted occasionally.

### Consequences are what the moves cost

Consequences exist on seventeen of the eighteen majors and on **none** of the sixty-four minors. They
are the mechanism that makes a major arcanum a different proposition.

**They are spent from inside the moves themselves** — a 7-9 choice, an alternative to spending a
follower's Loyalty, something the GM may impose on a miss. Not bookkeeping alongside the arcanum.

- One is marked when a move says so, or when a roll comes up a miss and the GM says so.
- **The player picks which.** Whatever it says, it happens.
- A nested consequence cannot be marked before the one it hangs under. Beyond that, any order.
- **There is no way to unmark one.** They are finite, and each marking spends an empty box.
- **Running out is a real state**, and what it means belongs to the arcanum. Some end with the thing
  broken or gone. Some require a consequence to be marked in order to use a move at all — and a
  character with none left simply cannot use it.

### What the consequences reach

Not damage. Permanent changes, in four directions:

- **The wielder.** Skin turns squamous; only raw flesh nourishes you; max HP cut and the instinct
  replaced with *"Hunger: to fill the emptiness inside you with excess"*; the ring fuses to bone and
  cannot be removed without taking the finger.
- **The world.** *1d6 sinkholes appear within a few miles of you*; an earthquake that is longer and
  more destructive each time it is chosen; a rift torn in reality with primordial power pouring
  through; an ancient being of Order that comes looking for you.
- **The people nearby.** *Name an NPC who is present and whose regard you value. They are terrified of
  the power you wield and grow distant.*
- **The village.** A storm that leaves the weather freakish for months, and the steading takes a
  penalty to its next roll to generate Surplus.

**Using a major arcanum is a bargain that gradually rewrites the person using it, and sometimes the
land around them.** It can also rewrite the arcanum: one consequence changes the thing's own cost to
*living, helpless, intelligent sacrifices*.

### What is true of them

- **It does not transfer the way a minor does.** *A magic spear that you bind to yourself with blood
  and fire isn't as easily shared as a spell written on a scroll.*
- **It can be inflicted.** A few represent a character being infused with power, and can arrive as a
  consequence of exposure to something potent rather than as a prize.
