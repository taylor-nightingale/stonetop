# Reference moves

The moves every character has. Nobody picks them, nobody loses them, and they are the same for
everyone in the game.

Distinct from playbook moves, which are chosen and differ from character to character.

---

## What they are

Twenty-six moves in four groups: ten **basic**, ten **expedition**, four **special**, two
**follower**. They are the game's verbs — the things anyone can do — and most of what a character
rolls is one of them.

Because they never change and everyone has them, a player learns them. After a few sessions a name
is enough to find one again and its trigger is enough to confirm it. **That is what makes a name
and a trigger sufficient to identify one**, and it is the property playbook moves lack.

## When each group is wanted

They are not wanted at the same times, and the differences are large.

**Basic moves — always.** Ten of them, and they trigger constantly. There is no moment in a session
when they are not about to be relevant. Nine of the ten roll.

**Expedition moves — while on an expedition, and phased within it.** They are not one set:

- *Setting out* — Outfit, Chart a Course, Requisition. Done before leaving, once, together.
- *On the road* — Have What You Need, Keep Company, Recover, Make Camp, Forage, Struggle as One.
- *Getting home* — Return Triumphant.

The first group is a checklist you work through at a single moment; the rest fire as the journey
does. Treating the ten as one set flattens a real distinction.

**Special moves — attached to something, not to a time.** Two different kinds sit in this group, and
they are not alike:

- *Rules about a roll.* Advantage/Disadvantage explains a mechanic rather than being invoked. Burn
  Brightly is the same kind of thing — a way of altering a roll — except that it is **conditional**:
  experience can only be spent on a roll when there is spare experience to spend. Both belong
  wherever a roll is being made or set up.
- *Events.* Death's Door fires when a character is dying. End of Session fires when the session
  does, and is how experience is gained. Each has a moment, and outside it is noise.

The conditional one is unlike the rest: whether it is available at all depends on how much spare
experience a character is holding, so it is a move that is simply absent most of the time.

**Follower moves — only when there are followers.** Strengthen Your Bond and Order Followers are
about followers and nothing else, and a character may have none at all.

## What is true of them

- **Nobody chooses them.** A character has them by existing, so nothing about them is a decision and
  there is no state to record about having one.
- **They are identical for every character**, which is what makes them learnable, and what makes an
  abbreviated form safe for them.
- **Most of them roll** — nine of the ten basic ones do — so they are the most-rolled moves in play.
- **Some carry resources.** Defend holds a track.
- **Two of them the book repeats rather than references.** Order Followers and Strengthen Your Bond
  are reprinted wherever followers are recorded, because a reader in the middle of giving an order
  cannot be expected to go looking.
