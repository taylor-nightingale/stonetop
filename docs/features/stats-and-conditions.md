# Stats and conditions

The six numbers a character rolls with, and the three states that hinder them.

---

## The stats

Six, in a fixed order: **Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, Wisdom, Constitution, Charisma**. Each is
a small number, usually between minus one and plus two.

They are **assigned once, from a set the playbook hands out.** Most playbooks offer the same spread —
one at +2, two at +1, two at +0 and one at -1 — and at least one offers a flatter set. A player
distributes those across the six however they like.

After that they barely move. A move taken at level-up can raise one by a point, to a ceiling of +2;
a rarer one lifts that ceiling to +3. Over a whole campaign a character might change two or three of
them.

**A stat is half of a roll.** The other half is the move. Neither is useful without the other, which
is why they cannot be put somewhere that makes them mutually exclusive.

## The conditions

Three: **weakened**, **dazed**, **miserable**. Each is two things at once — a description of how a
character is, and a mechanical consequence.

> *weakened* — Fatigued, tired, sluggish, shaky. Take disadvantage when rolling +STR or +DEX.
> *dazed* — Out of it, befuddled, not thinking clearly. Take disadvantage when rolling +INT or +WIS.
> *miserable* — Greatly distressed, angry, unwell, in pain. Take disadvantage when rolling +CON or
> +CHA.

They are **marked as a cost** — a move demands one, something goes badly — and **cleared by
fiction**: rest, care, time, or whatever the specific move asked for. They are not chosen the way a
move is chosen, and they are not permanent.

## The pairing

Each condition covers exactly two stats:

| | |
|---|---|
| weakened | Strength · Dexterity |
| dazed | Intelligence · Wisdom |
| miserable | Constitution · Charisma |

**The three pairs are contiguous in the canonical order, and between them they cover all six exactly
once.** Nothing is left out and nothing is covered twice. That is a property of the rules, not a
layout convenience, and it is what makes the relationship showable at all.

## A condition never compounds

Marking a second condition that touches the same roll changes nothing. A condition cancels advantage
if there is any, and otherwise sets disadvantage — it never stacks into something worse.

So *"how many of my conditions apply to this roll"* is not a question worth answering. Only *"does
one"*.

## What is true of them

- **A stat is read constantly and changed almost never.** A condition is changed during play and read
  constantly.
- **Both are needed to know what a roll will be**, and they live in different halves of the
  character: one is who you are, the other is how you are at this moment.
- **A condition is a sentence as much as a state.** "Dazed" without "out of it, befuddled, not
  thinking clearly" is a word with no fiction attached, and the fiction is what a player acts on.
- **Which stats a condition hinders has to be knowable before it is marked**, because marking one is
  frequently a choice between them, and the cost of that choice depends entirely on which of a
  character's numbers it touches.
- **The ceiling moves.** Plus two normally, plus three for a character who has taken the move that
  lifts it — so a cap cannot be assumed.
