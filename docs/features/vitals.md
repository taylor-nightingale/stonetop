# Vitals

Hit points, armor and damage. What a character can take, what they deflect, and what they deal.

---

## What they are

Three numbers that decide what happens when something lands.

**Hit points** are a pair. The current value moves constantly — it is the number that changes while a
player is looking at something else entirely. The maximum comes from the playbook and moves only in
rare circumstances; it does not climb with level.

**Armor** is a single number, and it comes from what a character is wearing. Worn armor does not
stack: only the best piece counts. Everything else that grants armor — a shield, a charm — adds on
top of it.

**Damage** is a **die**, not a quantity. A character's playbook grants it, it is rolled on its own,
and whatever moves or the fiction add is applied to the result. Stepping it up and down is
meaningless; it is a `d6`, not a 6.

## The shape all three share

Each of these is a **value a character owns, with an expectation beside it.**

The playbook says what maximum hit points and the damage die should be. The gear a character has
marked says what armor should be. But none of those are enforced, and none of them are stamped on
the value when it is written. What is stored is simply what someone decided.

So the relationship between the value and its source is **re-derived by comparison**: the expected
figure is worked out, set against what is actually there, and the answer is reported. A value that
matches its source is described as coming from it. A value that does not was typed in by hand, and
that is a normal thing to have happened — a GM ruling, a piece of fiction, a house rule.

This is guide-don't-enforce applied to numbers rather than to budgets. **The character sheet does not
compute these. It holds them, and explains them.**

## Why that matters

The obvious question about any of these three is *"why is my armor two?"* — and it has a real
answer, which is different for every character and changes as gear is marked and unmarked.

The answer is also **not color, not position, and not a number**. It is a sentence. Anything
presenting these has to be able to carry a sentence about each one, to everybody, without spending a
line of height on each — the answer is wanted occasionally and the numbers are wanted constantly.

## What is true of them

- **Hit points are a pair with two different rhythms.** Current changes many times a session; maximum
  changes a handful of times a campaign.
- **Damage is a die.** Not a number, not a range, and not something to increment.
- **armor is derived from gear but not driven by it.** Marking a breastplate does not make the
  number change by itself.
- **Worn armor does not stack**; only the best counts, and modifiers add to it.
- **Every one of the three can disagree with its source**, and disagreement is not an error.
- **Reaching zero hit points is a distinct state** with its own move attached to it, rather than
  simply being the bottom of a range.
- **They are consulted every time something lands**, which is often, and in the middle of something
  else.
