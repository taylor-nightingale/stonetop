# Inserts

A bundle of abilities granted to a character as a unit, most often because something happened to
them.

---

## What one is

**An insert is a fragment of a playbook.** It carries moves, it poses choices, and it may carry an
instinct — the same three things a playbook does — without being a whole character. There are no
stats in it, no background, no origin, no damage die. It attaches to a character who already has all
of that.

**The book's word is wider than this one.** What it prints as inserts includes a character's
inventory, a crew, an animal companion and a major arcanum — and each of those is its own thing here,
covered by `outfitting.md`, `followers.md` and `arcana.md`. A crew and an animal companion are
followers, and are treated as followers.

What is left over is what this covers: **a granted bundle of moves and choices that is none of
those.** The generic case, deliberately — a character's magic system, or the rules they continue
under after refusing to die.

## How one arrives

- **From a playbook, at creation.** One playbook's whole magic system is an insert.
- **From a background or a move.** Some are conditional on a choice made at creation.
- **From the GM, made for the occasion**, to represent a unique set of abilities a character picks
  up in play.
- **By dying.**

The last is the one that matters most, because it is the only route by which a character acquires a
whole new set of rules without choosing to.

## Dying is the main route

A character who dies and **refuses to pass through the Last Door** gains an insert, and **the manner
of the refusal decides which one**:

- *die but your soul lingers* — the soul stays without the body.
- *die but cling stubbornly to your body* — the body is kept.
- *die but call one of the Things Below by name and beseech them to intercede* — something answers,
  and now there is a master.

Each grants three or four moves and takes something immediately: the first two ask for a consequence
on the spot, the third for a mark.

**This is not a reward, and it is not a death.** It is a third state, entered at the worst moment, in
which the character continues under different rules.

## What an insert can do that nothing else can

**It can replace the character's instinct.** The instinct a playbook offers is chosen once and
changed only deliberately; an insert carrying its own overrides it. *Denial: to refuse to accept that
you are dead. Obsession. Fascination: to explore your powers, your master, your new existence.*

That is the sharpest thing about the feature. Every other grant adds to a character. An insert can
overwrite the single choice that says how they behave.

## The ledgers

The inserts gained by dying carry two lists, and neither is a menu of benefits.

**A terrible purpose.** One is chosen, and it names something specific: *the person or persons you
refuse to let go of*, *the person or persons who must pay*, *the task you refuse to leave undone*.
It is not flavour — it is how the character recovers. A night spent watching them, or raging over
them, or working at the task, restores everything. **The obsession is the healing.**

**Consequences.** One is taken on arrival, and another whenever a move says so. Eleven of them. Some
require another first — *Unstable* needs *Breakdown*, *Insatiable* needs *Strange Appetites*. They
run from a stench that makes animals shun you, to vermin nesting in your corpse who will do you
favours, to the attention of something called the Pale Hunter.

And the last one on the list ends the character: **their connection to humanity is lost and they
become a monster under the GM's control.** The ledger has a bottom, and reaching it is the end.

This is the same shape a major arcanum uses (`arcana.md`) — a finite, one-way list of permanent
changes, nested, ending in something final. Two unrelated features, one mechanism.

## Not all of them are ledgers

The insert granted at creation is a different animal: **a capacity that grows.** Two of its options
known at the start, one more at every even level, drawn from a fixed set of ten — and only one can be
running at a time, so the collection is a set of exclusive choices rather than an accumulation of
power.

It has no instinct, no moves of its own, and no consequences. What it has is a list of things the
character can do, most of which they cannot do yet.

## What is true of them

- **A character can hold more than one.** They do not replace each other, and one gained at creation
  is still there after one gained by dying.
- **An insert is not a playbook.** It never carries stats, hit points, a background, an origin or a
  damage die, and it never makes a character; it changes one.
- **What it grants is granted whole.** Unlike a playbook's moves, which are taken one at a time over
  a career, an insert's moves arrive together at the moment it does.
- **They can be written for one character.** A GM may make one to cover something that happened to
  one person and will never happen again.
- **Its instinct sets the playbook's aside; it does not erase it.** The playbook's choice is still
  there should the insert go.
- **One gained by dying brings the move made at zero hit points** in place of Death's Door —
  Tethered, Undying, Dark Succor.
