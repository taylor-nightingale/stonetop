# Outfitting

What a character is carrying, and what that costs them.

---

## What it is

Before an expedition a character commits to **how much they are carrying**. The total decides their
**load band** — light, normal or heavy — and the band is fiction, not bookkeeping: light is quick and
quiet, heavy is noisy, slow, hot and quick to tire.

It is not an inventory. The point is the trade.

## The thing that makes it unusual

**How much is decided up front. What it is, mostly, is not.**

Most of a load goes out as **unnamed marks** — weight carried without having said what it is. The
specifics are settled later, when the fiction asks for something: you need rope, so you had rope all
along, and that mark is now rope and can never be anything else.

So the trade is not made while packing. **It is made the third time you need something and have
nothing left to have been carrying.** Packing commits you to a weight; the choosing happens on the
road, one mark at a time.

That has a consequence which runs through everything else: **the list of items is not a packing
checklist, it is the menu of what an unnamed mark is allowed to become.** It is needed during an
expedition, not only before one.

## The list is not fixed, and writing in it is normal play

The printed list is not the whole of what a character can have. It comes with **blanks**, and filling
one in is ordinary: *the insert also has blank spaces, where you can write in other common items or
special possessions.*

**What gets written in is a full item**, no lesser than a printed one. It has a weight, or it is
small and has none. It can carry uses that are spent. It can carry tags, and a note saying what it
does. Nothing about being written in makes it a lesser citizen of the list.

**Things are acquired mid-expedition**, and that is the common reason to write one in: loot dug out
of a courtyard, a hide taken off something killed, a shard pocketed on the way past, gear handed over
by an ally.

**What is written in stays written in.** Whether a thing is still around next season, and whether
anyone else ends up with one, are questions for the table. An item a player wrote down is on that
character's list until that player takes it off. Nothing may clear it on their behalf — not the end
of an expedition, not setting a new load, not an entry looking unused.

## Three acts, two of which look identical

**Writing it down.** The item now exists on this character's list. Nothing is committed, nothing is
carried, and no load changes.

**Marking it** — acquiring. The character is carrying it, and the load goes up.

**Naming an unnamed mark** as it. Weight already committed resolves into this thing, and the load
does not change.

The last two end with the same item marked, which is why they are easy to confuse. They are not
equally common: **finding something mid-expedition is rare; mis-clicking is not.** Most changes to a
load after setting out are mistakes.

The first is the safe one, and its safety is the point: **writing something down never touches a
load and never spends an unnamed mark.** What a character has and what a character is carrying are
different questions, asked at different moments.

## The rules it lives under

- Three marks or fewer is **light**; four to six **normal**; seven and up **heavy**.
- Nine is the ceiling, and it is advice. Nothing forbids carrying more.
- **Regular items cost their weight.** A heavy thing costs more than one mark.
- **Small items cost nothing against the band.** They are tracked, and they do not make you heavier.
- **An unnamed mark costs exactly what a named one costs.** Leaving it open is not a discount.
- The steading's **prosperity** sets what some items are worth, and how many small items are
  available to take at all.

## What is true of it

- **One mark carries two facts at once** — what a thing costs, and that you have it. You learn the
  price by counting the marks you are about to fill.
- **Items are two kinds under two different rules**, not one list that got long.
- **A load is not permanent.** It is set for an expedition and set again for the next — and it is the
  **marks** that reset, never the list itself.
- **A character may carry something the printed list does not know about**, and writing it in is how
  that is handled rather than an exception to it.
- **Nothing enforces the limit.** Band and ceiling are both advice, and being over either is a normal
  situation, not an error to be corrected.
