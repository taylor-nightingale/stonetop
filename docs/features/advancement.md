# Advancement

Experience, level, and the moment a character becomes more than they were.

---

## What it is

A character marks **experience** constantly, in small amounts, mostly for failing. When enough has
accumulated they may **level up**: spend it, gain a level, and choose something new.

The cost of a level is **six plus twice the current level** — eight at first level, sixteen at fifth,
twenty-four at ninth. It grows every time, so each level is slower than the last, and a campaign is
perhaps eight of these moments in total.

## How experience is gained

**By missing.** The default across the game is that a failed roll marks experience. It is the steady
trickle, and it arrives at the worst moments, which is the point. A handful of moves say explicitly
that they do *not* mark on a miss; they are the exception and they say so.

**At the end of a session**, in a batch. A character marks for having demonstrated or struggled with
their instinct, and for a relationship that changed. Then the table answers four questions together —
did we learn about the world, defeat a threat, improve our standing, leave something lasting — and
every yes marks everyone.

So experience arrives two ways with completely different rhythms: a drip through play that nobody
plans, and a lump at the end that the whole table works out at once.

## What levelling requires

Two things, and only one of them is knowable from what a character has recorded.

- **Enough experience** — six plus twice the current level. Countable.
- **A quiet stretch of time at home.** Fiction, settled at the table. Nothing written down about a
  character can establish whether this is true, and it frequently is not: a character can carry
  enough experience for a level through an entire expedition without being able to spend it.

So *eligible* and *able* are different states, and only the first is knowable.

## What levelling does

It is a procedure, not a number going up:

- Subtract the cost from experience.
- Increase the level by one.
- **Choose a new move** — from the playbook, or from an insert class that has been unlocked.
- If the character is the Blessed, or carries a sacred pouch, and the new level is even: increase
  maximum Stock.
- If the character is the Lightbearer, or has Invoke the Sun God, and the new level is even: choose a
  new Invocation.
- **Review instinct and appearance.** Change anything that no longer applies; invent new options
  freely.

Two things follow from that list. **It reaches into nearly everything else** — a playbook move, a
possession's track, an insert's options, and two of the choices made at creation. And **it ends by
revisiting identity**, which means advancement is not only accumulation: a character is expected to
have changed, and to be able to say how.

The conditional steps depend on playbook or on something owned, so no two characters have the same
list.

## Experience at the threshold is contested

Once a character has enough to level, that experience becomes spendable another way: **two points
after any roll buys a single point on it.** It is only available at or above the level threshold.

So reaching the threshold is not simply progress. It is a standing choice between levelling and
burning, and every point burned pushes the level further away. A character sitting on enough
experience is holding a decision, not a milestone.

## What is true of it

- **Experience is gained by failing.** Whatever presents it is presenting the record of a bad day.
- **The threshold moves.** It is derived from level and grows with it, so it is never a fixed target.
- **Level is a small number that changes rarely** — perhaps eight times in a campaign.
- **Eligibility is knowable; permission is not.** The fictional half of the trigger lives at the
  table.
- **Levelling is a checklist with conditional steps**, some of which do not apply to a given
  character at all.
- **It finishes by asking who the character now is**, not by asking what they can now do.
